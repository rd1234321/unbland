Menu & rates

Every groom happens in the van, one dog at a time. Here is what each service includes, what it costs and how to pick the right one for your dog.

What each service covers

  • Bath & Brush

    A warm bath in the van, then a thorough brush-out. It suits dogs whose coats do not need cutting but do need a reset.

    Choose it for short coats, for dogs between full grooms, or when the main goal is a clean, fresh dog.

    from$65

  • Full Groom

    The bath and brush plus a trim to shape the coat. It is the right pick for breeds whose hair keeps growing, such as poodles, doodles and many terriers.

    The price starts at $95. Coat length and condition can change what a dog needs.

    from$95

  • Puppy Intro Groom

    A gentle first visit for young dogs. The aim is to teach a puppy that the tub, the brush and the dryer are nothing to worry about.

    It is a flat rate, and a good habit to start early.

    flat$55

  • Nail Trim

    Nails that touch the floor can change how a dog walks. A trim keeps paws comfortable and keeps scratches off your floors and legs.

    Add it to any groom for $20.

    add on$20

  • Teeth Brushing

    Plaque builds on dog teeth just as it does on ours. Brushing helps with doggy breath and supports healthy gums.

    Add it to any groom for $15.

    add on$15

Clean, brushed, home

Which one is right for your dog?

Short, smooth coat
A bath and brush is usually enough. Add a nail trim if you hear clicking on the floor.
Hair that never stops growing
Book a full groom. Without regular trims the coat tangles and mats, which is uncomfortable for the dog.
Under a year old
Start with the puppy intro groom. Early, gentle visits build a dog that accepts grooming for life.
Anxious or older dogs
A quiet van and one groomer make a difference. Say so when you book so the visit can go at the dog's pace.

Questions owners ask

How often should a dog be groomed?

It depends on the coat. Many dogs do well every four to eight weeks. Short coats can stretch longer.

Can I add services to a groom?

Yes. Nail trims and teeth brushing are add ons to any groom.

Why do prices say "from"?

Size, coat length and condition vary from dog to dog. The listed rate is the starting point.

Should I brush my dog before the visit?

A quick brush helps, but do not worry if you have not managed it. Tell Jess about any tangles when you book.

How it works

A grooming visit at your door is simpler than a trip to the salon. Here is what happens from the moment the van pulls up to the moment your dog is back inside.

One visit, start to finish

  1. The van parks at your home

    Jess parks in your driveway or at the curb. Your dog does not ride anywhere, and you stay close by.

  2. A quick hello and a check

    Jess meets your dog, looks over the coat and talks through what you want done. This is the time to mention sore spots, past bad experiences or anything your dog dislikes.

  3. Into the van, one dog at a time

    Your dog is the only dog inside. There are no cages and no other animals, so there is no barking from the next table.

  4. Warm bath, then the coat work

    The bath happens in the van with its own water. After that comes brushing and any trimming or add-ons you booked.

  5. Back to your door

    Your dog steps out clean and walks straight back into the house. You pay no mind to pickup times or waiting by the phone.

Everything on board

Water
The van carries its own water supply, so you do not need to run a hose or open up your bathroom.
Power
The van has its own power for the grooming equipment. You do not need to run an extension cord from the house.
A warm bath
The bath is warm, which matters for dogs that shiver or dislike cold water, and for older dogs with stiff joints.
Space
It is one groomer and one dog in a small, quiet room. That keeps the day calm for dogs that find salons loud.
60–90minutes, most visits

Most visits take 60 to 90 minutes. A small short-coated dog getting a bath and brush sits near the short end. A bigger dog or a full groom takes longer.

Coat condition, size and how patient your dog is on the day all change the time. Matted fur takes more work, and a nervous dog may need extra breaks.

Getting ready

Pick a spot

A flat driveway or a level curb with room for a full-size van works best. Think about shade and where the van can sit without blocking neighbors.

Let your dog out first

A short walk and a bathroom break before the visit helps a dog settle once grooming starts.

Tell Jess what you know

Share your dog's age, health issues, and anything that makes them nervous. It helps the visit go smoothly.
